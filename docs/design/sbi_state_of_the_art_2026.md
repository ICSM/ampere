## Summary: Ampere's GP niche holds; its SBI plumbing lags


Between January 2025 and October 2026 nobody published a method that puts a Gaussian-process residual model inside the simulator for neural simulation-based inference, and no SED, spectrum or retrieval code shipped a GP discrepancy term, so ampere's distinguishing construction remains unoccupied — and the one controlled JWST test of exactly that construction (Rotman et al., ApJ 2025) cut retrieved abundance offsets from **1.2–4.6 dex to 0.2–0.4 dex** while localising the model failure to one wavelength bin. Around that core, the field moved fast in four ways that ampere has not yet absorbed. The SBI ecosystem consolidated onto `sbi` 0.27.0 and BayesFlow 2.0.14, which now ship flow matching, VP-EDM diffusion, misspecification tests, test-time prior guidance, a tabular-foundation-model estimator and compositional hierarchical sampling, while swyft, lampe and LtU-ILI went dormant. Robust SBI split into three correction families — calibration-set (RoPE, FMCPE), learned-error-model (RVNP, minimum-distance summaries) and amortised generalised Bayes — whose data assumptions mostly do not fit a single-object fit; only the self-consistency loss (which needs exactly the evaluable likelihood ampere has) and ensemble disagreement transplant cleanly. On model comparison, the only controlled study of amortised evidence (Kucharský et al. 2025–26) finds that **posterior-based evidence routes consistently beat direct evidence networks**, and that self-consistency training repairs them only when a likelihood exists, while GPU-native nested sampling (blackjax nested slice sampling, TMLR 2026; jaxns 3.0) now supplies a ground truth at 20–40× CPU speed — both findings bear directly on the ENFR decision. The astrophysics software landscape has settled on importance-sampling efficiency plus IS-derived evidence as the certificate of a neural posterior (DINGO, BilbyFlow, Gebhard et al.), on ArviZ 1.x `DataTree` as the posterior format, and on bolting SBI onto legacy likelihood codes through wrapper packages rather than inside them. The highest-value additions for ampere, in order, are per-object IS efficiency and IS evidence under the amortised posterior, an ensemble-disagreement flag, self-consistency training on real spectra, conformal credible sets (CP4SBI), the `harmonic` learnt-harmonic-mean and Savage–Dickey estimators, GPU nested sampling on the jax backend, FMPE as the default head with online training for cheap emulators, Rotman-style localised bump kernels with an MSE validation metric, a Student-t likelihood for estimated covariances, and smolgp and GraphGP as reference solvers.

## Robust SBI: three correction families, two of which fit a single spectrum

The two reviews of the window set the frame. Kelly, Warne, Frazier, Nott, Gutmann and Drovandi's 46-page survey classifies misspecification-robust SBI into robust summary statistics, generalised Bayes and error-modelling/adjustment parameters, reports that tempering "is largely ineffective for synthetic and neural likelihoods", and lists as open problems standardised misspecification benchmarks, theory for neural conditional density estimators under misspecification, calibration of credible intervals and "robustness of flow-matching and diffusion-based SBI, which remain largely unexplored" ([Kelly et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12315)). The `sbi` maintainers' practical guide prescribes a pre-training detection step — a prior-predictive range check, an unconditional density estimate of p(x) flagging observations whose likelihood is "exceptionally low compared to all training samples", OOD detection in the embedding latent, and, "when likelihood evaluation is possible", the evidence p(x_o) by importance sampling with the posterior as proposal — and warns that neural SBI "can return erratic or spurious posteriors when data lies outside the model's range, rather than a sensible fallback or a broad posterior" ([Deistler et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.12939)). BayesFlow 2 adds the sharper statement that under a simulation gap an amortised estimator "can drift away from the Bayes posterior of the nominal model" so that q(θ|D) "may no longer correspond to any coherent Bayesian update", unlike MCMC ([Kühmichel et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07098)). Detection tooling has caught up with that advice: `sbi` 0.25.0 (5 September 2025) shipped `calc_misspecification_mmd()` (raw-data or embedding space, p-value against a reference distribution of MMDs among validation simulations) and `calc_misspecification_logprob()` (an unconditional `MarginalTrainer` density and a quantile test), with the tutorial showing a single observation too weak to flag misspecification while two pooled observations were flagged at p ≈ 0.012, and the marginal test unreliable when the marginal's own C2ST was 0.987 ([sbi tutorial 21](https://sbi.readthedocs.io/en/stable/advanced_tutorials/21_diagnostics_misspecification_checks.html)). The cheapest new per-observation detector is Alvey, Contaldi and Pieroni's deep-ensemble statistic: train N identical estimators, compute pairwise KL divergences, and flag Δ = KL_obs − KL_train significantly above zero; in their Gaussian toy model a threshold of ≈ 1.3×10⁻² corresponds to 1–2σ data deviations, "comparable" to a χ² residual test, with MIT code at github.com/james-alvey-42/sbi-ensemble-diagnostics ([Alvey et al. 2025–26](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13495v2)). Anau Montel, Alvey and Weniger's `mist` framework turns misspecification analysis into many hypothesis tests of parametrised distortions with a self-calibrating training algorithm, demonstrated on GW150914 (MIT, notebooks rather than a package) ([Anau Montel et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15100)).

Correction is where 2025–26 diverged, and each family carries a data assumption that must be checked against ampere's use. The calibration-set family needs real observations with known parameters. RoPE (ICML 2025) keeps NPE unchanged and adds an optimal-transport correction between learned representations of real and simulated observations using "a small real-world calibration set of ground-truth parameter measurements"; it is transductive and assumes conditional independence, and no official code was found ([Wehenkel et al. 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wehenkel25a.html)). Its inductive successor (NeurIPS 2025) merges calibration and alignment into one trainable model using paired calibration data plus unpaired real samples ([Senouf et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15593)). FMCPE (ICML 2026) learns a flow-matching transport of a simulation-trained posterior toward the posterior implied by N_cal ∈ {10, 50, 200, 1000} real (θ, y) pairs, drops RoPE's conditional-independence assumption, "consistently outperforms all baselines" in W₂ and C2ST across Gaussian, damped-pendulum, wind-tunnel and light-tunnel tasks, trains in 5–6 minutes per task, and has MIT code at github.com/pruhlmann/FMCPE (4 stars, pushed June 2026) ([Ruhlmann et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23385)). The learned-error-model family instead needs many unlabelled real observations of the same kind. RVNP (NeurIPS 2026 poster) jointly learns a posterior and an explicit Gaussian error model p_α(x_obs | x_sim, θ) = N(x_obs; x_sim, ξ(θ;α)), with ξ a θ-dependent diagonal plus a global off-diagonal term or a global Cholesky covariance, needs no misspecification hyperparameters, and is the one 2025–26 robust-SBI method tested on real astronomical data — **1,053 Gaia DR3 BP/RP spectra** with LAMOST labels and a MIST-isochrone synthetic simulator; it is overconfident at N_obs = 1, under-confident when the model is well specified, and on the spectra task its covariance "can collapse onto subsets of parameter space, and the metallicity cannot be recovered reliably"; no code repository was found ([O'Callaghan, Mandel & Gilmore 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05724v3)). Minimum-distance summaries adapt the summary statistic at test time by minimising an MMD between observed data and the summary-conditional predictive, need N = 100 i.i.d. observations, come with a Huber-contamination sensitivity bound, and do not match NNPE on SIR ([Khoo et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09161)). Preconditioned robust NPE importance-resamples training simulations around the observed summary and fits RNPE's spike-and-slab error model with NUTS denoising; on contaminated Weibull data (5% contamination, 100 replicates) it reports RMSE / 95% HPDI coverage of 0.09 / 0.98 against plain NPE's **1.01 / 0.00** and RNPE's 5.29 / 0.30, with MIT code ([Kelly et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18004v1)). The generalised-Bayes family makes power posteriors amortised: NSM-Bayes uses a weighted score-matching loss on a neural conditional-density surrogate, proves bias-robustness that NLE lacks, and on g-and-k with 10% outliers reports coverage of 100% (NSM-Bayes) and 100% (its conjugate closed-form variant at 5.1 s) against NLE's **0%**, at 2–3× NLE's training cost, with unlicensed code ([Bharti et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11325v1)); Sun, Nicholls and Lee (ICML 2026) condition a single NPE on the temperature β of the power posterior so that one network serves every tempering, but β remains user-specified and "principled temperature selection" is future work ([Sun et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22367v1)).

The method that fits ampere best is the one most SBI users cannot run. Mishra, Habermann, Schmitt, Radev and Bürkner's self-consistency loss adds, on unlabelled real observations x*, the variance over posterior draws of log p(x*|θ) + log p(θ) − log q(θ|x*), which is strictly proper only when the likelihood is evaluable; with four unlabelled observations it repairs NPE failures that 1,024 labelled simulations do not, on an air-passenger model it reduces mean bias from 0.079 to 0.014 (α) and 0.153 to 0.031 (β), and its stated limitation is that it needs fast density evaluation, so flow matching and diffusion are "less practical" ([Mishra et al. 2025–26](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13483v4)). The companion finding is that unsupervised domain adaptation and NNPE both "exhibit notably poor performance under prior misspecification" ([Elsemüller et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04949)). On the calibration side, CP4SBI provides finite-sample local conformal credible sets from the held-out simulations ampere already generates for SBC and TARP, is "statistically better on conditional coverage in 8 of 10 benchmarks" for NPE flows at a 10,000-simulation budget, and ships MIT code at github.com/Monoxido45/CP4SBI; its exchangeability guarantee is exactly what misspecification breaks, so it calibrates the amortiser, not the model ([Cabezas et al. 2025–26](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17077v2)). Posterior SBC extends calibration checking to run conditional on the observed data ([Säilynoja et al. 2026](https://paulbuerkner.com/publications/pdf/2026__Sailynoja_et_al__Statistics_and_Computing.pdf)), and a cosmology paper argues that prior-predictive coverage "only tests calibration on average": comparing NRE to likelihood-based inference at fixed parameters over 1,000 realisations, variances agree on average but not per realisation and kurtosis differs most ([Alokda, Porciani & Eggemeier 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00980)). No calibration-aware training loss newer than Falkiewicz et al. (NeurIPS 2023) surfaced, no balanced-NRE successor, and no cross-fitting work beyond Regier's CF-NPE. There is still no standardised misspecification benchmark; every paper uses its own contamination design, and the one recurring cross-paper pattern is plain NPE/NLE collapsing to 0% coverage under contamination with NNPE as the strongest simple baseline.

Read against ampere's premise — simulate from the GP-augmented forward model so the amortiser learns the residual-marginalised posterior — the literature calls this an NNPE-style corrupted-model approach with a structured correlated error model, and the closest evidence for and against it is RVNP: a free θ-dependent covariance on Gaia XP spectra collapsed and lost metallicity, which is independent evidence that the residual model needs a kernel-structured prior with few hyperparameters rather than a free covariance. The claim that "nobody has done GP-in-simulator SBI" rests on absence from searches under several phrasings, not on a review stating it; the nearest neighbours are RVNP, BayesFlow 2's NNPE augmentation module (2.0.6, July 2025) ([BayesFlow releases](https://github.com/bayesflow-org/bayesflow/releases)), HistFactory v2's unpublished GP-systematics slides ([Cranmer et al.](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1600677/contributions/6933567/attachments/3227709/5752730/HistFactory_v2_SBI.pdf)) and a kilonova paper using a GP emulator as the simulator ([arXiv:2605.13983](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2605.13983)).

**What ampere is missing / should consider (ranked):** (1) per-object IS evidence of x_o under the amortised posterior as the misspecification flag the practical guide names and almost nobody else can compute; (2) an ensemble-ΔKL flag and calibration-uncertainty estimate — K seeds in the artefact cache, no new maths; (3) self-consistency training on real spectra using the GP marginal likelihood, which constrains the density head to flows rather than FMPE; (4) CP4SBI conformal credible sets on the held-out SBC set; (5) `sbi`'s MMD and marginal-log-prob tests wired into the engine's pre-training step, with the documented caveat that single high-dimensional observations have weak power; (6) fixed-parameter frequentist shape checks (per-realisation variance, kurtosis) in the M2 validation design; (7) in `Population` mode only, FMCPE where benchmark stars with known parameters exist and RVNP-style learned error models as a comparison row; (8) RoPE, minimum-distance summaries and NSM-Bayes do not fit single-object fits and can be dropped.

## Posterior-based evidence beats direct evidence networks; GPU nested sampling becomes ground truth

The single controlled comparison of amortised model comparison in the window is Kucharský, Mishra, Habermann, Radev and Bürkner's study of four routes: NPE (marginal likelihood from prior × likelihood / posterior surrogate, needs a likelihood), NPLE (posterior and likelihood surrogates, likelihood-free), NEE (a network regressing the marginal likelihood directly) and NPMP (a classifier giving posterior model probabilities). Across a Gaussian marginal-likelihood case, a Gaussian Bayes-factor case, a racing-diffusion model on 17 participants' lexical-decision data and Eurostat air-traffic forecasting for 15 countries, **NPE and NPLE clearly beat NEE and NPMP, which remain poorly calibrated with or without self-consistency**; NPE with self-consistency on all 15 countries matches the Stan-plus-bridge-sampling gold standard, self-consistency on 8 countries improves but does not fix held-out countries, and in the racing-diffusion case NEE and NPMP sit at chance and were dropped. The recommendation is to "prefer parameter posterior-based methods and augment them with SC training on empirical datasets", NPE by default when likelihoods exist and NPLE when not, with NEE/NPMP "less attractive for reliable model comparison, especially under out-of-distribution conditions"; results are figures only, with no nats values in the text and no repository ([Kucharský et al. 2025–26](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20614v2)). ENFR (Lehman et al., submitted 16 October 2026) learns Bayes factors against a flow-matched reference and recovers absolute p(x|M) with a joint multi-model architecture; it was not in Kucharský's comparison and has no code link ([ENFR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23825)). Its ancestor, Evidence Networks, estimated Bayes factors only ([Jeffrey & Wandelt 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11241v2)), and Deep Bayes Factors (v3 December 2024) is likelihood-free and amortised but reports no numeric accuracy and has no code ([Kim & Rocková](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05411)). The only neural evidence route with a maintained package and a nested-sampling comparison in print is the harmonic lineage: the learnt harmonic mean with normalising flows is "in agreement with ground truth values and nested sampling estimates" up to 21 dimensions and needs only posterior samples from any sampler ([Polanska et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05969v3)), with a 37/39-dimensional Stage-IV cosmic-shear case going "from 8 months on 48 CPU cores to 2 days on 12 GPUs" ([Piras et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.12965)); Savage–Dickey density ratios with flows for nested models landed in `harmonic` 1.3.0 (June 2025) ([Lin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04339)), and `harmonic` 1.3.1 (November 2025) runs on numpy 2 and Python 3.11–3.13 under **GPL-3.0** with a search snippet suggesting an additional non-commercial condition that must be checked in the licence file before it becomes a dependency ([harmonic releases](https://github.com/astro-informatics/harmonic/releases)). floZ computes evidence from existing unnormalised posterior samples and their likelihood values, validated to 15-D against dynesty and reported "accurate" to 200-D on a Gaussian ([Srinivasan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12294)). Telescoping density-ratio estimation remains the reference for chaining classifiers once KL exceeds tens of nats, but no application to marginal likelihoods appeared in 2025–26 ([Rhodes et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.12204)). The one exotic item is program synthesis for SBI, in which an LLM proposes simulator programs scored by neural density estimation for joint model selection and parameter estimation ([Mishra-Sharma 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17540)).

The classical side changed more than the neural side. Yallup, Kroupa and Handley's nested slice sampling vectorises nested sampling with hit-and-run slice updates and a near-optimal slice-width rule, giving "accurate evidence estimates" and robustness on multimodal targets where tempered SMC struggles, published in TMLR 2026 with an open-source release ([NSS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23252)). A bilby-like acceptance-walk kernel inside blackjax-ns gives **20–40× speed-ups** on aligned-spin binary black holes with posteriors and evidences "statistically identical" to the CPU implementation ([Prathaban et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04336)), and GPU nested sampling with neural emulators delivered a 39-dimensional ΛCDM-versus-w0wa cosmic-shear Bayes factor "with a robust error bar" in about two days on one A100 ([Lovick et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13307)); the BEST sampler, updating several live points per step in TensorFlow, matched jaxns and UltraNest estimates while generally faster, recommending batches under ~10% of live points ([BEST 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28514)). Whether NSS lives upstream in blackjax (1.7.1, Apache-2.0, 1,135 stars) or in forks is unconfirmed; a GitHub code search returned one hit and downstream forks exist ([blackjax](https://github.com/blackjax-devs/blackjax)). jaxns reached 3.0.0 on 30 September 2026 (Apache-2.0) ([jaxns](https://pypi.org/project/jaxns/)). Among CPU samplers, dynesty 3.0.0 (4 October 2025) is a major refactor — samplers passed as classes, per-iteration convergence statistics saved, faster parallel uniform sampling, the Hamiltonian slice sampler removed — with 3.1.0 (July 2026) adding a duration estimate ([dynesty releases](https://github.com/joshspeagle/dynesty/releases)); UltraNest is at 4.6.3 (13 October 2026, GPL-3.0, with its GitHub releases page stale at 3.5.1) ([UltraNest](https://pypi.org/project/ultranest/)); nautilus is on maintenance releases at 1.0.6 (December 2025) ([nautilus](https://github.com/johannesulf/nautilus)); nessai is at 0.15.2 ([nessai](https://github.com/mj-will/nessai)); and **pocoMC has had no release since 1.2.6 on 20 September 2024** ([pocoMC](https://pypi.org/project/pocomc/)), which argues against adopting it now despite a NICER radius paper calling it its main sampler ([NICER J0437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08790)). No independent 2025–26 accuracy-versus-cost benchmark across these samplers exists; the accuracy claims for the GPU samplers are agreement statements, not nats tables. SunBURST offers deterministic GPU Laplace-style integration to near double precision for Gaussian-like posteriors up to 1,024-D but is not a general replacement for sampling ([SunBURST](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.19957)). The most-cited evidence results of the window are PolyChord re-analyses of DESI DR2: BAO plus Planck give ln B = −0.57 ± 0.26 modestly favouring ΛCDM against DESI's 3.1σ frequentist preference for w0waCDM, and replacing DES-SN5YR with DES-Dovekie eliminates the preference ([Ong, Yallup & Handley 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10631v2)); the follow-up across seven extensions finds "Bayesian Ockham's razor penalises extended models, yielding weaker or opposite preferences compared to Δχ²-based analyses" ([arXiv:2603.05472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05472)).

Information criteria and cross-validation moved with ArviZ: `arviz-stats` 1.3.3 (25 September 2026, Apache-2.0) carries PSIS-LOO on a `DataTree` ([arviz-stats](https://github.com/arviz-devs/arviz-stats)), and a secondary source reports `az.waic` removed in ArviZ 1.1 (verify against the changelog) ([skills.sh](https://www.skills.sh/pymc-labs/python-analytics-skills/model-evaluation)). The guidance on when evidence is the wrong question is unchanged: the marginal likelihood equals exhaustive leave-p-out cross-validation averaged over p and is prior-sensitive ([Fong & Holmes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.08737)), and Bayes factors are "very sensitive to features of the prior that have almost no effect on the posterior" ([Gelman 2023](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2023/10/14/bayes-factors-prior-cross-validation-posterior/)). No "LOO for SBI" paper and no 2025–26 astrophysics stacking or BMA methods paper was found; simulation-based stacking (AISTATS 2024) remains the nearest ([Yao et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/yao24b.html)). A design point for ampere rather than a literature finding: a GP likelihood is not pointwise-factorisable, so LOO must be the GP's conditional leave-one-out predictive, not PSIS over independent terms.

**What ampere is missing / should consider (ranked):** (1) make the NPE-marginal-likelihood route — GP marginal likelihood × prior / amortised posterior density, importance-corrected with the stored per-draw proposal densities — the primary amortised evidence, since it is the route Kucharský's evidence favours and ampere has every ingredient; (2) `harmonic`'s learnt harmonic mean and flow SDDR on NUTS/VI/emcee chains, subject to a GPL-3 licence check, covering two of the planned model-comparison module's items at once; (3) GPU nested sampling (jaxns 3.0 or blackjax NSS) as an inference engine on the jax backend and as the evidence ground truth for validating any amortised route; (4) dynesty 3's API lessons — sampler objects and persisted per-iteration convergence statistics; (5) `arviz-stats` PSIS-LOO with a GP-conditional LOO density, plus a stacking option with LOO weights for M-open cases; (6) drop pocoMC and Deep Bayes Factors; watch floZ and telescoping ratios.

## `sbi` 0.27 and BayesFlow 2 absorb flow matching, diffusion and hierarchy while swyft and lampe sleep

The ecosystem has consolidated onto two frameworks. `sbi` released 0.24.0 (March 2025: CategoricalMADE, score-based i.i.d. sampling, mini-sbibm), 0.25.0 (September 2025: unified `VectorFieldTrainer` for flow matching and score models, MNPE for mixed parameters, SNPE-B, transformer/causal-conv/LRU/ResNet/spectral-conv embeddings, the two misspecification tests, unconditional flow training), 0.26.0 (April 2026: `AmortizedVIPosterior`, diffusion guidance including PriorGuide, i.i.d. support for NPE via MCMC/VI/IS, EDM noise schedules, a 1-D time-series transformer, "FMPE with higher-dimensional conditions", the pyknos dependency and the PyTorch upper pin removed), 0.26.1 (PyMC and Pyro made optional extras; the TARP z-scoring bug fixed and the default `num_bins` changed) and 0.27.0 (4 August 2026: NPE-PFN, scale-equivariant standardisation for FMPE/NPSE, an LC2ST refactor, legacy aliases deprecated with removal planned for 0.28); the repository is Apache-2.0 with 876 stars and was pushed on 21 October 2026 ([sbi releases](https://github.com/sbi-dev/sbi/releases)). The software paper is in JOSS ([sbi reloaded](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.07754.pdf)). BayesFlow went 2.0.7 (August 2025) to 2.0.14 (1 September 2026), adding score-based diffusion and NNPE augmentation (2.0.6), diffusion guidance and conditional OT flow matching (2.0.8), a major refactor with consistency models, contrastive NRE, a graphical approximator for hierarchical models and ensembles (2.0.9), compositional sampling for hierarchical models (2.0.11), a PyMC wrapper and modernised transformers (2.0.12), `ModelComparisonWorkflow`, TARP and Diffusion Transformers (2.0.13), and revised defaults plus a C2ST with conformal testing (2.0.14) — with serialisation breaks at 2.0.8→2.0.9 ("pin to 2.0.8 if needed") and 2.0.14, MIT, 781 stars, Keras 3 over jax/torch/tf, and **Python pinned to ≥3.12,<3.14** ([BayesFlow releases](https://github.com/bayesflow-org/bayesflow/releases); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/bayesflow/json)). The rest is dormant or small: swyft's last release is 0.4.5 (September 2023) with the last push in March 2024 ([swyft](https://pypi.org/pypi/swyft/json)); lampe's "development has stopped in favor of the sbi project in July 2024" ([sbi reloaded](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17337v1)); LtU-ILI has no PyPI package, no releases since v0.1.0 (November 2024) and **pins `sbi` ≤ 0.22.0** ([LtU-ILI releases](https://github.com/maho3/ltu-ili/releases)); the jax-native options are sbijax 0.4.2 (October 2026, 55 stars) and GenSBI 0.4.1 (Flax NNX, Simformer/Flux1Joint, sbibm C2ST 0.50–0.56, 20 stars) ([sbijax](https://pypi.org/pypi/sbijax/json); [GenSBI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27499)), while zuko 1.6.0 and flowjax 19.1.2 are maintained flow libraries rather than SBI frameworks ([zuko](https://pypi.org/pypi/zuko/json); [flowjax](https://pypi.org/pypi/flowjax/json)). No head-to-head package benchmark exists since LtU-ILI's 2024 paper.

On backbones, the TMLR tutorial review by Arruda, Bracher, Köthe, Hasenauer and Radev gives the first controlled head-to-head, all in BayesFlow. On ten sbibm tasks the VP-EDM schedule is best overall, flow matching "competitive but dropped slightly on the smallest problems", an adaptive two-step SDE solver both fastest and most accurate, and a diffusion-transformer backbone gave no clear gain over an MLP at about 3.5× the cost; on a 72-parameter PEtab ODE benchmark with over 27,000 measurements, EDM-VP with F-parameterisation ranked first (NRMSE 0.42, ECE 0.03), uniform and OT flow matching ranked 10th–11th (NRMSE 0.39, ECE 0.07), discrete and continuous consistency models 13th and 15th (ECE 0.11 and 0.14), ε-prediction diverged in about 5% of samples, and the full amortised pipeline ran under one day against roughly three days of MCMC for one dataset; on Gaussian random fields up to 256×256, consistency models were worst and degraded with grid size while flow matching and diffusion were robust and benefited from online training with 2.5 million fresh simulations. The recommendations are VP-EDM by default, v or F parameterisation, adaptive higher-order solvers, no Langevin or predictor–corrector, a calibration check whenever guidance is used, flow matching as the "easier to implement" choice "with fewer hyperparameters", and consistency models only for latency-critical use, "not the default for high-dimensional SBI" ([Arruda et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20685v5)). The practical guide agrees: spline flows are the default NPE head, flow matching trains faster and more stably but samples in 50–100 network evaluations, score-based sampling takes "seconds rather than milliseconds", flows are preferred for NLE because invertibility gives cheap likelihoods, and below about 100 parameters and 100 data dimensions CPU training "can be as fast as or even faster than on a GPU" for batches under 1,000 ([Deistler et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12939)).

On tokenised and transformer estimators, ampere's coordinate–value–mask encoding is the same design as Simformer's and TFMPE's. TFMPE (April 2026) tokenises each parameter and observation value with a variable id, intra-variable position, group id and a Gaussian-Fourier-encoded continuous coordinate, runs an encoder-only transformer with full self-attention, and reports usable planning numbers: attention memory O(B·T²·h), **≈1.4 GB per training step at 1,304 tokens** (100 sites, 2 blocks, latent 256), extrapolating to ≈135 GB at 1,000 sites, with an MLP vector field recommended for many more sites; on hierarchical benchmarks its ℓ-C2ST of ≈2.5×10⁻³ beats flattened NPE at ≈0.18, and "NUTS did not converge reliably beyond five sites" on a 100-site seasonal SEIR ([Charles et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20723)). PriorGuide (ICLR 2026) adapts a trained diffusion SBI model to a new prior at test time "without costly retraining" and is in `sbi` 0.26.0 ([Yang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13763)). NPE-PFN uses TabPFN as a conditional density estimator with no training or architecture selection, "sometimes needing orders of magnitude fewer simulations", and is in `sbi` 0.27.0 ([Vetter et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17660)); its dimension limit is unstated and every in-context estimator of the window (Distribution Transformers, ACE, metabeta) is validated at ≲10–20 parameters. A microlensing paper shares one transformer encoder between an evidence network and an NPE over irregularly sampled time series, reaching 99.9% detection efficiency at a false-positive rate below 6×10⁻⁴ on simulated Roman data ([Smyth et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20260)). The guide's warning stands: transformers "may require a prohibitively large number of simulations" on small budgets.

Training protocols moved towards spending simulations better. Multifidelity NPE (ICLR 2026) transfers from cheap low-fidelity to expensive high-fidelity simulators with "up to two orders of magnitude fewer high-fidelity simulations", with no package found ([Krouglova et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08416)); multilevel neural SBI (NeurIPS 2025) applies multilevel Monte Carlo ideas across simulator fidelities ([Hikida et al. 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/118621)). The guide's tuning rules are explicit: validation NLL as the objective because it upper-bounds the posterior KL, early stopping and checkpointing, "invest in early rounds" of TSNPE, "most successful applications have required at least a few thousand simulations", with no correlation between parameter count and simulation count across 106 published applications, and a five-seed ensemble that "showed good calibration" where each member was "slightly overconfident" ([Deistler et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12939)). Online training with fresh batches "is not susceptible to overfitting because each simulation is seen only once" ([BayesFlow 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07098)). Application papers report that lower validation loss does not guarantee tighter posteriors (shallower flows had lower loss but broader posteriors) ([Hough & Bloom 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19919)) and that NPE "is sensitive to the choice of architecture and training hyperparameters" ([Zelmer et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27439)).

Hierarchical and population SBI has three routes. Compositional score matching with an error-damping estimator (ICLR 2026) is numerically stable to 100,000 data points, uses "less than one full model simulation" on larger problems, scales to a microscopy inverse problem with over 750,000 parameters, and shipped in BayesFlow 2.0.11; the review's evidence-accumulation case shows the damping factor trading sharpness against calibration with "more groups generally need more damping" ([Arruda et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14429)). Tokenised factorisation (TFMPE, posterior factorisation) is attention-bound at about 100 sites. The astronomy workhorse remains importance-reweighting amortised per-object posteriors into a population likelihood, and 2026 quantified its failure mode: 2×10³ posterior samples per event "are not enough for reliable hierarchical inference" ([arXiv:2606.14229](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14229)), reweighting "can introduce severe Monte Carlo noise" when per-event posteriors are poorly constrained ([arXiv:2607.00075](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.00075)), and a strong-lensing NRE pipeline adds a post-hoc coverage calibration before combining 100 lenses to reach 22.8% on w and 2.9% on Ω_m ([Jarugula et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23534)). Dingo-Pop replaces the whole chain with a transformer over per-event tokens that returns a population posterior in ~1 s, "free of per-event Monte Carlo sampling noise" ([Leyde et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11274)). `sbi` itself has no hierarchical API; BayesFlow 2 does.

**What ampere is missing / should consider (ranked):** (1) FMPE as the default upgrade from spline flows and VP-EDM NPSE with v/F parameterisation as the option for ≥50-parameter posteriors, exposing only the review's recommended defaults; (2) an online fresh-batch training loop for cheap emulators, which `sbi`'s offline `append_simulations` API does not provide natively; (3) PriorGuide for per-object or per-population prior changes without retraining; (4) a per-object draw budget far above 2×10³ and per-object coverage calibration before population reweighting, with the Monte-Carlo-noise diagnostic exposed; (5) multifidelity NPE across the emulator-versus-full-model axis, ampere-side since no package exists; (6) keep the transformer in the embedding and the density head small, with TFMPE's memory figures as the planning rule; (7) NPE-PFN as a zero-training baseline for ≤10-parameter sub-problems; (8) track `sbi`'s 0.28 alias removals; do not depend on LtU-ILI, swyft or lampe; watch BayesFlow 2 on jax for compositional hierarchy and GenSBI/sbijax as single-maintainer projects.

## Rotman et al. validate the GP-residual thesis at 0.2–0.4 dex while smolgp and GraphGP extend the solvers

The strongest 2025–26 evidence for ampere's core construction comes from exoplanet retrievals. Rotman, Welbanks, Line, McGill, Radica and Nixon put a GP on the residual r = d − m(λ) with K = white noise + a global kernel + a sum of localised Gaussian-bump kernels with free amplitude, length scale and centre, and in injection–recovery on a synthetic WASP-96b NIRISS/SOSS spectrum the traditional retrieval's median offsets of **1.24 dex (H2O), 1.89 dex (CO2) and 4.64 dex (CH4)** fell to 0.19 / 0.37 / 0.20 dex with the combined global-plus-local GP; the localised kernel recovered an injected 1.65 µm feature's centre to within ~0.01 µm, one bin width; on real WASP-96b the local kernels flagged mismatches near 1.18 µm and 2.7 µm with the GP preferred at 4.8σ; a Matérn-3/2 gave results similar to the squared exponential; and when no correlated noise was injected the traditional model was preferred by over 3σ, so the GP is not selected when it is not needed. They propose MSE (bias² + variance) as the metric for comparing retrieval set-ups, built the covariance on celerite and george inside Aurora with MultiNest, and published no repository or run times ([Rotman et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21702v2)). The stellar-spectroscopy motivation is now quantified: fitting 7,087 GALAH DR4 spectra, Buder and Różański find **4.5% of 13,927 pixels exceed 3σ against ~0.3% expected for Gaussian noise**, 380 significant residual regions, and data-driven models trained on the residuals alone still recover T_eff to 80–150 K and [Fe/H] to 0.06–0.09 dex — structured, learnable misspecification, though modelled with a Cannon-style residual model rather than a GP ([Buder & Różański 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25583)). PITCHFORK brings the GP-as-nuisance pattern to asteroseismology with an emulator reaching T_eff to 5.88 K and 35 radial-mode frequencies at 0.02% ([Scutt et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02926)), a 2-D time×wavelength GP argues the standard per-channel approach wrongly assumes uncertainties uncorrelated in wavelength ([Manickavasaham et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20423)), and cosmology's "theoretical-error likelihood" marginalises an unknown theory error into an augmented covariance, tracing its failures to an inadequate cross-covariance between probes — the same mathematics as a joint multi-channel discrepancy term ([arXiv:2410.08930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08930)). Every one of these papers hand-rolls the construction on george or celerite; no reusable, backend-agnostic implementation with localisation diagnostics exists, and no paper applies a GP discrepancy to stellar parameter fitting or galaxy SED fitting with a bias or coverage study.

Generalised Bayes produced three automatic learning-rate selectors and a bagging result, none astronomy-specific. Onizuka's closed-form rate minimises a weighted Fisher divergence between the asymptotic general posterior and a normal with sandwich covariance, needing only score and Hessian estimates, and "tends to mitigate undercoverage under model misspecification" ([Onizuka 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26478)); Lee, Liu and Nicholls show the learning rate cannot be learned from the same data and use held-out data with ELPPD and coverage utilities ([Lee et al. 2025–26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12532)); variational bagging extends BayesBag to VI with a Bernstein–von Mises-type theorem that recovers off-diagonal covariance even under mean-field and "stays robust under model misspecification" ([Fan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20594)). For spectra with 10³–10⁴ correlated pixels a scalar temperature cannot say where the model is wrong, so these complement rather than replace a GP discrepancy. Diagnostics beyond posterior predictive checks remain PSIS-LOO Pareto-k (k ≥ 1 read as "failed/likely severe misspecification", p_loo far above the parameter count as misspecification, with the caveat that high k also arises from flexible well-specified models) ([loo](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/loo/loo.pdf)), and robust pre-fit screening now has an astronomy implementation: Hilder, Hogg, Casey and Rix's robust heteroskedastic matrix factorisation implicitly maximises a Student-t likelihood, produces per-pixel robust weights as a continuous outlier measure, was applied to Gaia DR3 RVS, and comes with a "fast JAX implementation" (Robusta-HMF), accepted in ApJ ([Hilder et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08081)) — this is the RHMF that ampere's deferred W5.16 trial anticipated, now with a reference implementation.

The solver landscape has one direct successor to ampere's jax quasiseparable scan. smolgp (Rubenzahl, Hattori, Särkkä, Farr, Luhn, Bedell and Foreman-Mackey, AJ 2026) is a tinygp-compatible JAX state-space GP using `jax.lax.associative_scan` for O(N/T + log T) GPU cost, with an augmented integral state that gives "exactly the same posterior as a fully integrated GP" for finite exposures at machine precision in float64, and with state-space kernels beyond quasiseparable such as the quasiperiodic ([smolgp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02527); [repo](https://github.com/smolgp-dev/smolgp)); celerite2's only 2026 release (0.3.3, July) is compatibility maintenance, so the Foreman-Mackey group's new development is there ([celerite2](https://github.com/exoplanet-dev/celerite2/releases/tag/v0.3.3)). For images, GraphGP is the first astronomy-built differentiable GPU Vecchia — bit-reversed k-d-tree ordering, CUDA forward/inverse/log-determinant/kernel gradients, linear time and memory "to nearly a billion parameters" — with no timings or repository on the abstract page ([Dodge, Frank & Clark 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11402)); the EFGP line's 2024 extension to non-stationary kernels with log-linear scaling is the one to read for ampere's warped kernels in 2-D ([arXiv:2407.03608](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03608)); and gallifrey does Bayesian kernel-structure learning by SMC in JAX, the posterior-over-structures alternative to ampere's horseshoe over kernel sums, not yet scalable to 10⁴-pixel spectra ([Boettner 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20394)).

Emulators with error models have numbers at last. Różański and Ting's scaling laws for TransformerPayne show a tenfold increase in training compute yields ~7× lower MSE, requiring ~2.5× more data and ~3.8× larger model, with √MSE the typical flux uncertainty ([Różański & Ting 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18617)); SE3D is a Bayesian-neural-network dust-radiative-transfer emulator at ~0.05 dex, the closest analogue of ampere's planned RT emulator, without stating how its spread enters a likelihood ([Ramnichal et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19623)); and a 2026 asteroseismology benchmark proposes that an emulator is reliable only if residuals fall below the survey's observational uncertainty ([arXiv:2609.03004](https://pith.science/paper/2609.03004)). The "emulator error in the likelihood" practice is most explicit in cosmology: a Euclid bispectrum emulator adds a covariance from prediction residuals to the simulation covariance with a Student-t-style likelihood for the noisy estimate ([arXiv:2506.18974](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18974)), and Heavens, Whiteway and Sellentin derive a multivariate Student-t approximation, matched on covariance and multivariate kurtosis, for the convolution of the Sellentin–Heavens likelihood with extra Gaussian error sources explicitly including emulator uncertainties ([Heavens et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19463)). In every example found the emulator covariance is fixed; nobody in 2025–26 propagates a parameter-dependent emulator error through a likelihood with a coverage validation.

**What ampere is missing / should consider (ranked):** (1) localised Gaussian-bump kernels with free centres as a documented kernel-sum recipe, where the horseshoe already does the "how many bumps" selection Rotman et al. did by Bayes factors, plus a named absorbed-power-per-region statistic and the MSE metric in M2; (2) RHMF wrapped from Robusta-HMF for `ampere.diagnostics` rather than re-derived; (3) a Student-t likelihood class for `FixedCovarianceNoise` when the covariance is estimated, with the emulator residual covariance added to it and the Różański–Ting / residuals-below-survey-error rules as the drop-or-model criterion; (4) smolgp benchmarked against the W7 jax scan provider and its exposure-integration and quasiperiodic kernels adopted or cited; (5) PSIS-LOO Pareto-k maps per wavelength using the GP's conditional LOO; (6) Onizuka's closed-form learning rate and block-bootstrap variational bagging as cheap post-hoc cross-checks of the GP-marginalised posterior; (7) GraphGP as a watch item for the image modality and gallifrey as an M2 comparison row; (8) the PITCHFORK caveat — a wrong kernel can absorb real signal — named in the docs.

## Astrophysics codes bolt SBI on through wrappers and certify it by importance-sampling efficiency

Across galaxy SED fitting, retrieval and stellar spectroscopy the flagship likelihood codes added physics and sampler plumbing but not SBI, emulators or discrepancy models. Prospector's only in-window release is 1.4.1 (January 2026, numpy 2), and its `NoiseModelKDE` and `Kernel` classes for correlated uncertainties are the nearest thing to a misspecification tool in any SED code ([Prospector releases](https://github.com/bd-j/prospector/releases)); Bagpipes made five releases to 1.3.6 with no readable changelog ([Bagpipes](https://pypi.org/project/bagpipes/)); CIGALE added a `Nebular_AGN` module ([arXiv:2606.14643](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14643)). SBI arrived through wrappers: synference uses `synthesizer` as the forward model and LtU-ILI for training, picked a neural spline flow over an MDN after ~3,000 Optuna trials, trained on 10⁶ simulations with a JADES-matched noise model, runs SBC, TARP and coverage automatically, and fits **3,088 JADES galaxies in ~3 minutes on one CPU, ~1,700× faster than dynesty's ~90 minutes per galaxy**, with "slightly bell-shaped" rank histograms and a simulation stage that "can accept models from other codes" including Bagpipes and Prospector ([Harvey et al. 2026](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/547/1/stag282/8472650)); its forward model `cosmos-synthesizer` made 20 releases in the window ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/cosmos-synthesizer/)). FrankenBlast found SBI++ posteriors "better calibrated than Prospector nested sampling, though neither method was perfectly calibrated" ([arXiv:2509.08874](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.08874)). In retrieval, petitRADTRANS 3.3.0 (January 2026) added **covariance matrices for `Data` objects** — the community only now admitting correlated noise — and pRT4 is an experimental JAX rewrite "not numerically identical to version 3" ([pRT changelog](https://gitlab.com/mauricemolli/petitRADTRANS/-/raw/master/CHANGELOG.md); [pRT4](https://petitradtrans.readthedocs.io/en/jax_dev/content/pRT4_changes_description.html)); FASTER demonstrates amortised retrieval plus model comparison via posterior model probabilities that "match those computed using nested sampling" on WASP-39b ([Lueber et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18045)); Gebhard et al. set the expectation that importance sampling "successfully corrects inaccurate ML results, identifies model failures via low efficiencies, and provides accurate estimates of the Bayesian evidence", with FMPE training ~3× faster than NPE at higher IS efficiency ([Gebhard et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21477)). In stellar spectroscopy, Payne Zero argues an emulator "is no longer required for point-estimate fitting" but that sampled inference at 10⁴–10⁶ likelihood evaluations "remains a setting where an emulator is the practical choice" ([arXiv:2607.24141](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24141)), and the amortised-NPE package `nbi` has been dormant since December 2023 ([PyPI nbi](https://pypi.org/project/nbi/)).

Gravitational-wave astronomy shows what research-to-production looks like. GWTC-4.0's catalogue of record is still Bilby plus dynesty with calibration parameters sampled alongside binary parameters ([GWTC-4.0 methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18081)); Bilby 2.6.0 fixed a long-standing PSD-normalisation error that silently overestimated the likelihood for time-domain Tukey-windowed data, and 2.7.0 moved to the dynesty 3 API with plugin samplers ([bilby CHANGELOG](https://github.com/bilby-dev/bilby/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)). DINGO made 22 releases from 0.9.0 to 0.10.3, adding calibration handling and network-selection logic "for ASIMOV", an `lvk-stable` maintenance line, the Dingo-T1 transformer embedding, multi-GPU training, cached log-likelihoods for importance sampling and reproducible random streams ([dingo releases](https://github.com/dingo-gw/dingo/releases)), and DINGO-BNS reached Nature with ~1 s binary-neutron-star inference against ~1 h for the fastest traditional methods ([PMC11882463](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11882463)). BilbyFlow puts NPE inside the Bilby suite and reports the metric that now certifies a neural posterior: on 38 high-mass GWTC-3 events, **29/38 reach IS efficiency above 1%** (3 min–1.5 h), the rest fall far below (up to ~35 h), with a median efficiency of 7%, "roughly comparable to DINGO" ([Pinchbeck et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00766)). Jim (jax, flowMC, ripple) is at 0.5.2 and pre-1.0 ([jimgw](https://pypi.org/project/jimgw)). In cosmology, emulators-in-likelihood is the production path — CosmoPower emulators validated for Euclid DR1 with CLOE integration planned ([DiRAC](https://dirac.ac.uk/cosmopower-emulators-for-euclid-dr1-cosmological-inference/)), CLOE interfaced to Cobaya and CosmoSIS with Metropolis–Hastings and PolyChord ([CLOE 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22475)) — while SBI runs real analyses (DESI DR2 distances plus Planck through LtU-ILI ([arXiv:2512.17744](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17744)); GalSBI on DES Y3 ([Fischbacher et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23783))) without being any Stage-IV release's pipeline of record.

Population inference at scale has two production patterns. pop-cosmos learns a score-based diffusion prior over 16 SPS parameters and uses it as the prior for per-galaxy GPU MCMC with the Speculator emulator at **~8.2 GPU-seconds per galaxy over ~4 million KiDS-1000 galaxies**, with redshift bias 2×10⁻³ and σ_MAD 0.03 against DESI BGS, and its authors note posteriors under the population prior are "systematically slightly overconfident" ([Halder et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03930); [Thorp et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12122v1)). LSST DESC's NPE cataloguing on DC2 coadds "outperforms the standard LSST pipeline in light source detection, flux measurement, star/galaxy classification, and galaxy shape measurement" with the authors expecting "some model misspecification … with real LSST images" ([Duan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15315)). Neural variational population posteriors with smoothed importance sampling give goodness-of-fit and evidence in seconds with up to 10³× fewer likelihood evaluations, valid only if posterior support is covered ([Mould, Wolfe & Vitale 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07197)). The two-stage per-object-then-NumPyro-NUTS-on-GPU pattern is the pragmatic baseline users expect ([arXiv:2606.28501](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.28501)). Gaia DR4 is fixed for 2 December 2026 ([Gaia news](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/news)); Rubin DP1's photo-z used RAIL's eight algorithms at σ_NMAD ~0.03 with ~10% outliers ([RAIL DP1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07370)); Euclid Q1's NNPZ returns modes and quantiles, not posteriors ([Euclid Q1 PHZ](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15306)). No columnar posterior-store standard exists.

Formats and norms: ArviZ 1.0.0 (2 March 2026) replaced `InferenceData` with `xarray.DataTree` and split into arviz-base/arviz-stats/arviz-plots, 1.1.0 emits a `MigrationWarning` on importing `InferenceData`, pre-1.0 NetCDF/Zarr files remain readable, and PyMC 6 adopted it ([arviz releases](https://github.com/arviz-devs/arviz/releases); [migration guide](https://python.arviz.org/en/latest/user_guide/migration_guide.html)); specutils 2.0 renamed `Spectrum1D` to `Spectrum` and 2.2 requires astropy ≥ 7 ([specutils](https://specutils.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)); astropy 8.0.0 landed 16 June 2026 ([astropy](https://pypi.org/project/astropy/)); speclite 1.0.0 is the live filter library while sedpy's last release is from 2020 ([speclite](https://pypi.org/project/speclite/); [sedpy](https://pypi.org/project/sedpy/)). The de-facto SBI validation report in 2026 astronomy is the practical guide's chain — prior-predictive check, pre-training misspecification test, posterior predictive checks, global coverage (expected HPD coverage, SBC, TARP), then local coverage (L-C2ST, posterior SBC), with "passing global checks is necessary but not sufficient" — plus, where a likelihood exists, IS efficiency and IS evidence ([Deistler et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.12939v1)). AAS software policy did not change in the window; a JOSS companion paper remains the expected route ([AAS policy](https://journals.aas.org/policy-statement-on-software/)).

**What ampere is missing / should consider (ranked):** (1) per-object IS efficiency and IS evidence under the amortised posterior as the SBIEngine's headline accuracy metric, as DINGO, BilbyFlow and Gebhard et al. report it; (2) interop with `synthesizer` as a simulator source and synference as the amortised-SED benchmark to compare against (1,700× speed, automated TARP/SBC); (3) speclite 1.0 as the filter library and Prospector-β priors as a prior family, rather than reimplementation; (4) a learned population prior (diffusion or flow, pop-cosmos style) as a prior node, and the pop-cosmos overconfidence number as a calibration target; (5) DINGO's production lessons — cached log-likelihoods, reproducible random streams, an `lvk-stable`-style maintenance branch — and Bilby's normalisation regression test; (6) hard compatibility checks against specutils 2's `Spectrum`, astropy 8 and ArviZ 1.x `DataTree`; (7) pRT4's jax backend as a future foreign-function or emulator source; (8) do not depend on LtU-ILI, `nbi`, sedpy or pocoMC.

## Findings that bear on the ENFR and CF-NPE decisions

First, the only controlled evidence on amortised evidence estimation points away from direct networks. Kucharský et al. find NPE-derived marginal likelihoods and NPLE beat NEE and NPMP in every case, that NEE/NPMP are "poorly calibrated with or without SC", and that self-consistency repairs the posterior-based routes only when a likelihood is evaluable ([Kucharský et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20614v2)). ENFR is a direct evidence network with a flow-matched reference that recovers absolute p(x|M); it was not in that comparison and its out-of-distribution calibration is exactly the failure mode documented for NEE. Ampere always has a likelihood (the GP marginal), so the posterior route — amortised posterior as proposal, GP marginal likelihood × prior as target, the stored per-draw proposal densities for the correction — is the evidence-backed default, and ENFR must beat it on the same problems to earn its place. This also answers the output-shape question an evidence engine raises: the robust output is a posterior density from which evidence derives, not a regressed scalar.

Second, the harmonic lineage is the only neural evidence route with a maintained package and a nested-sampling comparison in print, and its SDDR-with-flows addition already covers the Savage–Dickey item ampere has ruled ([Polanska et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05969v3); [Lin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04339)); the GPL-3 licence and a possible non-commercial clause are the only blockers to check ([harmonic](https://pypi.org/project/harmonic)).

Third, GPU nested sampling is now a practical ground truth: "statistically identical" evidences at 20–40× CPU speed ([Prathaban et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04336)), a 39-D Bayes factor in two days on one A100 ([Lovick et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13307)), jaxns 3.0 and blackjax NSS both Apache-2.0 on ampere's jax backend. Any ENFR trial should be scored in nats against this, not against dynesty on a toy; no paper in the window reports nats-level accuracy for a direct evidence network.

Fourth, no published work puts a GP residual inside the simulator for SBI, and the nearest neighbour, RVNP on 1,053 Gaia XP spectra, shows a free covariance collapsing and losing metallicity ([O'Callaghan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05724v3)). Ampere's premise is unoccupied and the kernel-structured residual versus free-covariance contrast on the same XP task is a publishable result that the ENFR/CF-NPE work can share a training set with.

Fifth, the self-consistency loss is the natural companion to CF-NPE because both exploit an evaluable likelihood that most SBI users lack, but it needs fast density evaluation and is "less practical" with flow matching or diffusion ([Mishra et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13483v4)); adopting FMPE as the default head and self-consistency training are in tension, which the engine design should resolve explicitly (flows where SC is on, FMPE elsewhere). No cross-fitting work beyond CF-NPE was found, and no calibration-aware loss newer than 2023, so CF-NPE has no published competitor on its own ground; the nearest alternatives on the calibration side are CP4SBI's conformal sets and ensembles.

Sixth, the practical guide names IS evidence under the amortised posterior as a pre-training misspecification route "when likelihood evaluation is possible" ([Deistler et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.12939)), and DINGO, BilbyFlow and Gebhard et al. make IS efficiency the certificate of a neural posterior; whichever evidence path ampere chooses, the per-object IS efficiency should be reported alongside it, and a BilbyFlow-style tail (24% of events below 1% efficiency) is the realistic expectation on real spectra.

Seventh, two test-design findings bear on how CF-NPE's claims are validated: prior-predictive coverage "only tests calibration on average" and per-realisation variance and kurtosis can disagree with likelihood-based inference even when coverage passes ([Alokda et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00980)), and the population-level NRE pipeline found a post-hoc coverage calibration necessary before combining per-object ratios ([Jarugula et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23534)). Eighth, single-classifier ratio estimation fails once the KL exceeds tens of nats ([Rhodes et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.12204)), a relevant bound for any ENFR configuration that compares models with strongly separated evidences.

## Conclusion

The window's clearest lesson is asymmetric: the part of ampere that is hardest to build — a backend-agnostic GP discrepancy likelihood with scalable solvers — faces no competitor and gained its first independent quantitative validation, while the part that is easiest to assemble from the ecosystem — the amortised engine's heads, diagnostics, calibration and certification — has moved on in ways that are cheap to absorb and expensive to ignore. The practical consequence is that the ENFR and CF-NPE decisions should be framed around what ampere uniquely has, an evaluable likelihood: that single fact makes the posterior-based evidence route the evidence-backed default, makes the self-consistency loss and per-object IS certification available where the rest of the SBI world cannot use them, and turns ENFR into something that must prove itself in nats against GPU nested sampling rather than something ampere needs. The same fact resolves a design tension the field has not: FMPE is the right default head everywhere except where a density-evaluable flow is needed for self-consistency training, and the engine should say so.

What changes going forward is the validation standard rather than the architecture. A 2026 reader of an ampere paper will expect the practical guide's diagnostic chain, IS efficiencies per object, coverage checked per realisation rather than on average, and a Bayes factor whose reference is a GPU nested sampler; the M2 study's existing agreement rows can carry those, and Rotman et al.'s MSE metric and localised-kernel diagnostics give the GP side an equally concrete reporting form. The open scientific question the literature leaves for ampere is the one RVNP stumbled on: whether a kernel-structured residual model keeps the posterior identifiable where a free learned covariance collapses, and ampere is the only package positioned to answer it on real spectra.
